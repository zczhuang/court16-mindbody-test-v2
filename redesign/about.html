Our mission

Make tennis a place
you belong.

We use metaphors, cross-sport drills, and creative teaching to make tennis more welcoming and accessible — without losing what makes it great.

What we stand for

Three pillars hold this up.

01

Member-centered culture

Every decision starts with the member experience — not the calendar, not the spreadsheet. We assemble the finest talent and lead with knowledge, kindness, and humility.

02

Lifelong learning

It's not a race to a certain ball or court. It's a journey to a deep understanding of the game. We design for sustained progress, not vanity milestones.

03

Community building

Tennis is supposed to be social. We design clubs that feel like a third place — somewhere you'd come even if you weren't playing that day.

The origin

2014. Downtown Brooklyn. One court.

Court 16 was founded on a simple frustration: tennis lessons were either too rigid or too aimless. The first club opened in Downtown Brooklyn in 2014 with a method we called Tennis Remixed — borrowing drills from soccer, basketball, and music education to teach tennis better.

Eleven years later, that method runs through six clubs across NYC, Philly, and Boston. Same idea, scaled. Award-winning facilities (the Brooklyn flagship won an iF Design Award in 2025) but the same emphasis on people first.

The Tennis Remixed method

Borrow from every sport. Stay true to one.

Cross-sport drills

Soccer footwork. Basketball reads.

We pull movement patterns from other sports because they teach the body things tennis-only drills don't.

Metaphor over mechanics

"Catch the egg." Not joint angles.

Mechanical instruction creates self-conscious players. Metaphors create instinctive ones.

Music-class energy

Repetition with variation.

Like learning an instrument — repeat the same skill with small variations until it becomes second nature.

Sound of Tennis

Adaptive tennis for blind & visually impaired players.

Court 16 Newton is the home of Sound of Tennis — an adaptive program using audio cues, modified balls, and trained coaches to make tennis accessible to players with vision loss. The first program of its kind in New England.
