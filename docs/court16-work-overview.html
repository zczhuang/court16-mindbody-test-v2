Everything we’ve built, and what to do next

A complete index of the Court 16 codebase and consulting work — eight repositories, five shipped packages, three unbuilt proposals — followed by a recommended order of attack.

8 repositories ~27,800 lines of application code $11,500 of $15,000 SOW shipped 4 live deployments

The sign-up flow is finished and smoke-tested against real MindBody, and it is not taking bookings. Five of five test scenarios passed against production Ridge Hill on 22 May. Since then it has sat behind a single environment flag, waiting on about 45 minutes of HubSpot clicks that nobody has done. That is the whole gap between built and earning.

Everything else in this portfolio is either downstream of that flip or waiting on the same kind of unlock — a two-minute admin toggle in MindBody, a ten-second checkbox in HubSpot. The engineering is not the bottleneck and hasn’t been for months. Six owner-actions, none longer than an hour, stand between the current state and a six-club booking system with revenue reporting on top of it.

Two corrections

Things that aren’t what they sound like

There is no mobile app.

Nothing native exists — no React Native, Expo or Capacitor anywhere in the eight repositories. Every surface is responsive web. Real mobile engineering did happen (the redesign’s nav was reworked across roughly a dozen commits for small screens), but there is no App Store deliverable and none was scoped. If a native app is wanted, that is a new conversation, not a finishing touch.

The marketing spend audit doesn’t exist yet — and that’s the finding.

Three capability audits were done: a live tag audit of court16.com on 18 June, a probe of what HubSpot’s API will and won’t return on the current tier, and a map of the MindBody revenue endpoints. What none of them could produce is an audit of spend, for a simple reason: ad spend is not connected to any system Court 16 owns. It lives inside Meta and Google and reaches nothing else. Spend in the campaign console is a number somebody types in.

So the honest position is that marketing investment is currently unauditable, not un-audited. You can see who opened an email; you cannot see whether a campaign produced a single member or a single dollar. Priorities 4 through 6 below are the fix, in the order that costs least.

Recommendation

What to pursue, in order

Ordered by return per hour of Court 16’s time, not by how interesting the work is. The first two are almost entirely other people’s clicks.

1

Go live at Ridge Hill

Highest return, lowest cost, already paid for

Everything is built and it passed five of five scenarios against production MindBody on 22 May — book, staff confirm, reassign, deny, and the 24-hour reminder trigger. Three bugs were caught and fixed in that session. Since then the app has been sitting in test mode.

What stands in the way is Ibtissam’s four workflow toggles and about 45 minutes of copy-paste, then one environment flag on our side. Nothing engineering-side is pending. Every week this waits is a week of trial signups still going through the twelve-field form and the two-to-five-day silence.

Needs: Ibtissam · ~45 min then Cedarwind · one flag
2

Roll out the remaining five clubs

Turns a pilot into the business case — and unlocks everything below it

The same four-step pattern that landed at Ridge Hill, repeated: authorize the API source, create the trial program and $0 service, populate the schedule, then we capture the IDs and run the same five-scenario smoke. Roughly 25 minutes of admin per club, split across Anthony, Jane and club staff.

Do this early even if the pilot is still settling, because it is also a hard prerequisite for measurement: five of the six clubs return no API data at all until their source is authorized. Every revenue and attribution item below is blocked on this step for five-sixths of the business.

Needs: Anthony · 10 min total Jane · 50 min total Club staff · scheduling
3

Resolve adult payment

The one genuinely broken customer path

The adult intro cart link drops people on MindBody’s Enrollments tab showing “No Scheduled Workshops” instead of a checkout. We diagnosed it and tried the modern hosted-cart URL; Ridge Hill returned 404 on every endpoint because the site isn’t provisioned with MindBody’s Branded Web Tools. A comparable site redirects correctly, which confirms the diagnosis. It’s reverted to the wrong-tab-but-loads state and filed with MindBody Support.

Two possible gates, and they may both apply: Branded Web Tools provisioning, and the sellable-online flags on the three adult services. If Support stalls, the documented fallback is MindBody’s Healcode embeddable widgets — about 15 minutes per club, and it gets us the fully-embedded checkout we originally wanted. The kids trial is unaffected; don’t let this hold up items 1 or 2.

Needs: Anthony · MindBody Support + sellable-online flags
4

Start the attribution clock

Hours of clicks, no code — and the cost of waiting is permanent

Confirm or create GA4, deploy the HubSpot and GA4 tags on the booking subdomain, add a self-referral exclusion, and remove the dead Universal Analytics tag that’s been collecting nothing since 2023. That’s the whole of phase zero.

It ranks this high for one reason: the GA4 export to BigQuery is not retroactive. History only starts accruing the day it’s switched on, so every week of delay is a week you can never analyze later. It also lights up HubSpot source attribution that is already coded and dormant — the booking form reads and forwards the HubSpot visitor cookie today, but the cookie is never set on the booking subdomain, so the field arrives empty.

Needs: Anthony · GA4 access Cedarwind · hours, not days
5

Make the money visible

The first time anyone can say “this club drove N members and $X”

Two to three weeks. The only genuinely new engineering is the MindBody read side — sales, visits and contracts per club — because the authentication, token caching and pagination all already exist. Output is dollars per club: new members, new-member revenue, monthly recurring revenue, trial-to-member rate, blended acquisition cost and marketing efficiency ratio.

Deliberately no data warehouse at this stage. The whole dataset is two tables and a few tens of thousands of rows; Postgres and SQL views answer it for essentially nothing. We’d graduate to BigQuery when ad spend makes it a real multi-source join — not before.

Needs: item 2 complete for all six clubs ~$0–5/month to run
6

Connect spend and close the loop

Where the ads start optimizing on members instead of form-fills

Manual and CSV spend entry first — it ships in week one with no integrations, and it’s the permanent home for the print, event and signage spend no ad API will ever see. Then the ROI view in the campaign console, where the existing double-down / investigate / cut engine finally has dollars behind it rather than email opens.

Then the payoff: when MindBody confirms a membership, push that back to Google Ads as a conversion so bidding optimizes on real members. The Ads account is already tagged, so this is thinner than it sounds. One thing to start now regardless — apply for the Google developer token; the 5-to-14-day approval is the long pole on the whole sequence.

Needs: Anthony · Google Ads access + who runs the ads a UTM naming convention everyone follows

One standing decision, alongside all of the above

The court16-booking-ideal fork removes the manual staff-confirm click entirely — the parent creates their MindBody account inside the wizard through OAuth, the booking confirms immediately, and staff get an FYI with an escape-hatch deny link instead of a task. It has a clean end-to-end smoke run behind it.

That directly changes the acceptance criteria for priority 1, which currently promises 95% of submissions confirmed within one business day — a bar that depends on staff clicking promptly. The fork makes it structural instead. It’s a real architectural choice and it deserves a decision rather than continuing to sit as a parallel branch.

Deliberately deferred

What we’re not doing, and why

  • Statistical media-mix modellingWants 104+ weeks of clean weekly data and costs around $50k/year from a vendor. Six clubs, no spend history. The manual spend table in priority 6 is the on-ramp; revisit in roughly two years. The realistic advanced step is a geo-holdout test — the six clubs are natural test cells, which is a cleaner read on causality than any attribution model.
  • Server-side tag managementIts main benefit doesn’t apply here — attribution is handed off to the HubSpot contact at form submit, so the cookie only needs to survive minutes. Revisit when paid spend is large enough to pay for the server.
  • Meta conversions APINo Meta pixel exists on the site. Google goes first because the Ads account is already tagged.
  • The redesign as a real projectEleven polished pages exist and they’re live, but they were built as a realistic host for the booking flow, not as a committed site rebuild. Worth a decision — it shouldn’t drift as an unowned prototype.
  • Fuzzy identity matchingOnly email and the hard MindBody-to-HubSpot key for now. Falsely merging two real members is worse than missing a match, so name-based matching goes to a human review queue if it’s ever needed.
